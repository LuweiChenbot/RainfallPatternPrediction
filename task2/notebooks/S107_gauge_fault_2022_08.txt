S107 (East Coast Parkway) gauge fault, 19-22 August 2022
=========================================================

Sources
  Raw readings : data/HistoricalRainfallacrossSingapore2022.csv
  Stations     : task1_revision/data/matrix_5min/stations.csv
  Task 1 files : outputs/task1/extreme_5min_readings.csv, outputs/task1/extreme_complete_days.csv

Summary
  From about 19 Aug 23:20 to 22 Aug 03:45 the S107 gauge reported heavy rain
  that did not fall. Neighbouring stations stayed dry the whole time. Before
  and after this period S107 agrees with its neighbours, so the gauge is
  normally healthy.

1. Location and neighbours
  S107 East Coast Parkway: 103.9625 E, 1.3135 N
  Nearest stations active in August 2022 (distance from S107):
    S207 Bedok Road          1.3 km
    S212 Tampines Ave 9      3.0 km
    S84  Simei Ave           3.9 km
    S224 Airport Blvd        4.1 km
    S208 Changi East Close   4.5 km
    S61  Chai Chee           4.7 km
    S113, S94, S24, S78, S215, S29, S43, S214, S221 at 6-9 km
  S207, only 1.3 km away, is the key witness: real rain at S107 should also
  appear there.

2. Timeline: S107 vs S207 (1.3 km)
  19 Aug  S107: showers 08:40-09:05 (3.8 mm) and 13:00-13:40 (8.6 mm)
          S207: same two showers at the same times (10.7 and 14.3 mm)
          -> healthy. 23:20-23:45: S107 5.4 mm while S207 is dry (first odd readings)
  20 Aug  S107: rain almost all day, 262 mm.   S207: two single tips, 0.4 mm
          -> faulty
  21 Aug  S107: almost continuous 00:00-23:45, 786.6 mm.
          S207: one real shower 03:10-04:05, 15.1 mm
          -> faulty
  22 Aug  S107: bursts until 03:45, about 11 mm.   S207: dry
          -> end of the fault
  23-24 Aug  Both stations record the same showers at the same times
          (e.g. 24 Aug 12:00: 4.0 vs 4.4 mm; 18:35: 5.4 vs 4.8 mm)
          -> healthy again

3. Close-up: 21 Aug, 01:00-03:00
  S107 reported rain in all 24 consecutive 5-minute slots (mm):
    01:00-01:55  4.4  3.2  8.6 11.8  8.6  6.8 12.6 13.0  7.2 12.0 14.6 15.6  = 118.4 mm
    02:00-02:55 11.2 14.2 13.6 10.4  8.2 14.0 13.4  9.8 10.4  9.2 13.2 13.8  = 141.4 mm
  S207 (1.3 km): 0.0 in all 24 slots.  S212 (3 km): a single 0.2 mm tip.
  260 mm in two hours (about 130 mm/h sustained) with zero rain 1.3 km away
  is not physically plausible: a storm that intense covers kilometres and
  rises and fades within about an hour.

  Strongest evidence: 03:10-04:05 real rain reached the area. S207 recorded a
  genuine 15.1 mm shower (3.0-3.6 mm per 5 min), while S107 went almost silent
  (0.4 mm). S107 was not tracking the weather at all.

4. Close-up: the record 85.6 mm label (S107, 21 Aug 19:55)
  The label is the sum of the next six readings:
    20:00  20:05  20:10  20:15  20:20  20:25
     4.4 +  5.4 + 21.8 +  7.8 + 37.2 +  9.0  = 85.6 mm
  S107 was dry until 19:55, spiked suddenly, and was dry again by 20:30.
  S207, S212, S84, S224 and S208 (1.3-4.5 km) all read 0.0 in every slot
  from 19:30 to 20:55.
  37.2 mm at 20:20 is the largest single 5-minute reading anywhere in 2017-2024.
  The 8 largest labels in the dataset (74.8-85.6 mm in 30 minutes) all come
  from this fault, from two bursts around 01:25-01:45 and 19:50-20:05.

5. Nature of the fault
  - Not a data-pipeline problem: every reading arrived 9-10 minutes after its
    slot, like the neighbours' readings, so it was not a delayed batch or a
    backfill.
  - The gauge itself recorded the bucket tips in real time. Tipping-bucket
    gauges can register false tips from mechanical or electrical faults, or
    from water reaching the bucket that is not rain. The data cannot tell
    which.
  - Odd values such as 8.60001, 9.39999 and 8.39999 appear only on the fault
    days (normal readings are clean multiples of 0.2). Not proof by itself.

6. Effects on the project
  Labels       : the 8 largest 30-minute labels (74.8-85.6 mm) are fake. The
                 largest genuine total is 71.8 mm (S64, Nov 2018), confirmed
                 by neighbouring stations.
  Mapper       : the "class 8 runs to 85.6 mm" tail comes from the broken
                 gauge; every class-8 value above 71.8 mm is fake.
  Tier 1       : the maxima of t1_rain_1440 (787.4 mm), t1_rain_120 (266.6 mm)
                 and t1_max5_30 (37.2 mm) all come from these two days.
  Tier 2       : the fault looks like "extreme rain at one station while every
                 neighbour is dry". Left in, it teaches the model that isolated
                 extreme rain happens with dry neighbours, weakening the
                 neighbour signal Tier 2 is meant to learn.
  Task 1       : in outputs/task1/extreme_5min_readings.csv the two largest
                 5-minute readings (37.2 and 29.4 mm) and 8 of the top 11 come
                 from this S107 fault or from S36's phantom-rain days
                 (2020-2021). The audit's "maxima up to 37.2 mm" refers to this
                 fault. S107's 21 Aug escaped extreme_complete_days.csv only
                 because it had 283 of 288 readings; S36's 1 Apr 2021
                 (319.6 mm while the island was dry) still tops that table.

7. Cleaning recommendation
  Masking whole days 20-21 Aug leaves about 16 mm of fake readings
  (5.4 mm on the night of 19 Aug, about 11 mm early on 22 Aug).
  Recommended mask for S107: 2022-08-19 22:00 to 2022-08-22 06:00.
  This removes the whole fault and keeps S107's genuine showers on the
  morning and afternoon of 19 Aug. Masking whole days 19-22 Aug also works,
  but loses those two real showers.
