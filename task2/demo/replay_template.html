Rain in the next 30 minutes: Singapore, 2024

For every rain gauge and every five minutes of 2024, the chance that more than a chosen amount of rain falls there in the next 30 minutes, as forecast by the final random forest from the readings up to that moment, next to the rain that actually fell. 2024 is the test year: the model never saw it while being trained or tuned.     How this page works. No model runs in your browser. The page stores what notebooks 09 and 10 produced: for every station and five-minute step of 2024, the final random forest's chance of more than 0, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10 and 20 mm in the next 30 minutes (rounded to 0.4%), and the rain that then fell. A threshold between those values is filled in from how the rain was spread within each band in 2017–2023, exactly as task2/code/predict.py does.

    Running the model itself. python task2/code/predict.py S90 "2024-12-29 16:40" 5 loads the saved forest (or --model LR, the logistic regression), builds the inputs from the feature tables in task2/data and forecasts any station and time of 2017–2024. Only 2024 was held out; earlier years were used for training.

    The forecasts. The forest was trained with Spark MLlib on 2017–2023 and uses 58 inputs: the station, the clock, the station's own rain over the past minutes to day, and the rain at neighbouring stations, upwind and how it is changing. Persistence is the reference: how often more than the threshold followed when the previous 30 minutes had rain in the same band (none, up to 0.2 mm, 0.2–0.5 mm and so on), in 2017–2023.

    No forecast means the station has no reading at that time or one of its next six readings is missing, the same rule as in training and testing. The last forecast of the year is made at 23:25 on 31 December. Times are Singapore time; a time between readings uses the latest reading before it.
